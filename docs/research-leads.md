# Research leads: historical context

Leads to follow up, not yet used. Each needs checking against its primary source before it is cited.

## Royal Engineers Museum, Library and Archive, Gillingham

- Brompton Barracks, Gillingham, Kent. Corps library from 1813; research centre with archive stores opened 2021.
- Ask whether it holds Board of Ordnance or Royal Engineers records of the Rotunda's re-erection at Woolwich, 1818 to 1820, to set beside the Royal Artillery Museum at Larkhill and The National Archives.
- Douglas Galton, Lectures to Royal Engineers at Chatham.

## Galton and the ventilating fireplace

- Sir Douglas Galton (1822–1899), Royal Military Academy, Woolwich, then Royal Engineers. Designed the Herbert Hospital, Woolwich.
- The Galton ventilating grate, adopted by the War Department: outside air admitted to a chamber behind the fire, warmed, and supplied to the room to replace the air drawn up the chimney. The fireplace treated as a ventilation system.
- Sources: D. Galton, Healthy Hospitals (1893); Address on the General Principles which should be Observed in the Construction of Hospitals (1869), appendix on ventilating fireplaces.
- Relevance: flues as extract paths in historic houses; nineteenth-century practice at Woolwich.

## Congreve, gas and fuel

- Sir William Congreve, 2nd Baronet: Home Office inspector of the metropolitan gas works, report 1822–1823. His office and expenses were challenged in the Commons: HC Deb 2 April 1824, vol. 11, cc. 107–12.
- General manager of the Imperial Continental Gas Association from 1824 (records at The National Archives / London Metropolitan Archives, to check).
- Patent for chalk, limestone and other calcareous substances to save fuel; printed report of experiments, 1819 (Science Museum Group).
- Reported to have opened gas works in European cities with George Landmann. Source to find.

## Landmann

- George Thomas Landmann (1779/80–1854), born at Woolwich, son of Isaac Landmann, professor of artillery and fortification at the Royal Military Academy. Royal Engineers; engineer of the London and Greenwich Railway (1836).
- Drawings by Isaac Landmann on arch construction in fortifications reported in the Royal Artillery archive.

## Royal Engineers and building technology

- J. M. Weiler, Army Architects: The Royal Engineers and the Development of Building Technology in the Nineteenth Century, PhD thesis, University of York (White Rose eTheses). Chapters on cast iron, wide-span roofs and fireproof construction; context for the Rotunda's iron columns.

## Highcombe House occupants

- William Henry Barlow FRS (1812–1902), engineer of the St Pancras train shed, lived and died at High Combe, 145 Charlton Road (English Heritage plaque, 1991). His Royal Society papers include work on telegraph earth currents and the flexure of beams.
- Sir George Whitmore (1775–1862), Royal Engineers, possibly resident after 1840 (Bone 2013 says "likely"). Unconfirmed.
